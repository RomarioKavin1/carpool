# Claims audit — every quantitative and factual claim, re-run

**Verdict: the measurements are sound and reproduce exactly; the *guard* that is
supposed to stop them drifting does not work, and six claims are outright
false.** Every figure in `docs/AB-MEASUREMENT.md`,
`apps/bench/src/redo-measured.json` and `docs/PHASE0.md` re-derived correctly
from its named source, and Phase 0's pair sample even regenerates byte-for-byte
from its stated seed. But the test the doc calls "the only reason a recorded
number is allowed to sit in the repo" is a substring check that passes with the
doc claiming the exact fabricated figure (**143** sources) that commit `9ef4062`
retracted — and `9ef4062`'s own message claims "a doc cannot drift from the run
in either direction", which is false. That is the third instance of the repo's
founding defect and it is in the fix for the first. The outright false numbers
are `apps/registry/README.md`'s **~25 MB** model download (it is **87 MB**, and
the repo states the right figure elsewhere), `measured.ts`'s
**436,700 µUSDC** (the gate charged **436,745**), and four wire claims in
`CONTRACT.md`.

Audited on branch `feat/phase1-reproducibility` at `b99e22a`, Node 20.19.0.

### The `embed.test.ts` flake (M7), diagnosed

It did not reproduce: **10/10** runs of `packages/carpool-tracker/src/embed.test.ts`
alone and **25/25** runs of the whole tracker suite were green on Node 20.19.0,
and the test has no timing, network, randomness or shared state — every case
injects `loadPipeline`, so the failing case is `fakePipeline(new
Float32Array(384))` against `dim: 128`, which is deterministic.

The recorded symptom is the diagnosis: "expects a rejection and got a 384-wide
`Float32Array` of zeros" is exactly what `embed()` returns if the
`data.length !== dim` check is not taken. Copying `embed.ts` and `embed.test.ts`
to a scratch directory and replacing that check with `if (false)` reproduces the
failure verbatim, same test name, 1 failed | 4 passed. Both audits recorded that
other agents were mutating production files in this window and restoring them
(`rank.ts`, `pricing.ts`, `gate.ts`, `settler.ts` are all named in N1, and
`docs/AUDIT-TESTS.md` describes exactly this method). **The red run was almost
certainly a concurrent mutation probe, not a flake** — the same conclusion the
claims audit already drew for the transient `rank.test.ts` failures, not applied
to this one. Nothing was changed in `packages/carpool-tracker` (outside this
pass's paths) and nothing needs to be.

### M4 — the price fraction is 0.15 in the economics and 0.10 in the runner

`docs/PHASE0.md` §5 derives break-even (15%) and the operating target (40%) from

40%, and the precision@1 bars that follow (95% / 36% / 61%) all move. Neither
document notes that the other exists. `0.15` is also the fraction the derived
spend cap rests on, so the two live side by side in shipped code.

#### Resolved — 0.10, 2026-09-12

**The decision.** 0.10, in one constant. Recorded here in summary; the argument
lives on `PRICE_SHARE_OF_REDO_COST` in `packages/carpool-core/src/pricing.ts`,
because that is the file someone changing it will open.

**Why 0.10 and not 0.15.** Every bar in §5 is a function of this constant, so the
two candidates were compared on §5's own algebra against Phase 0's own measured
`P(substitutable) = 0.42`:

| | share 0.10 | share 0.15 |
|---|---|---|
| break-even `P(useful)` | 10% | 15% |
| operating target | 30% | 40% |
| precision@1 needed at target | **71%** | **95%** |
| precision@1 needed at break-even | 24% | 36% |
| precision@1 with partial credit (f = 0.4) | 46% | 61% |

At 0.15 the product clears its own operating target only at **95% precision@1**
— near-perfect retrieval over open-domain research questions, which this repo has
never measured (`pnpm bench`'s 1.000 is a synthetic corpus with no topically-close
wrong answer in it, and says so in its own banner). 71% is demanding and
unmeasured too, but it is a bar retrieval systems reach. Three further reasons,
in order of weight:

1. **The self-report.** `estimatedCostUsd` comes from the author, which is the
   entire reason price is a *fraction* of it. The fraction therefore **is** the
   strength of that guard: an author who doubles their claimed cost extracts 20%
   of the true cost at 0.10 and 30% at 0.15.
2. **The measured erosion.** Re-running the A/B guard with the share at 0.15 drops
   the recorded saving from **9.1× to 6.1×** on this repo's one measured artifact
   (397,041 → 595,562 µUSDC against a $3.97 redo floor). A third of the headline
   saving, and the saving is the pitch.
3. **Nothing had to be regenerated.** 0.10 is the share the one executed
   measurement was taken under. Choosing 0.15 would have meant reprinting a real
   measurement's figures because a policy input moved — the exact shape of the
   defect this repo has retracted twice.

**What 0.10 costs, and what would reverse it.** The author side gets worse: 10
sales at the base price to repay production against ~7 at 0.15, and fewer than
either in practice because `priceAt()` decays toward `priceFloor`. With a measured
median of 7.5 free substitutes per event the paid window is short, so 10 sales may
never arrive. That is a real argument for 0.15, and it is rejected for one reason:
it is arithmetic over a quantity this repo has never measured — there is no sales
data in the tree. The buyer side has measurements (0.42; a 0.5-day median window);
the supply side has none. A measured precision@1 ≥ 95% over a real corpus, or
observed authors quitting below ~10 sales, would reopen it. Both are measurements,
not edits.

**What was changed.** `PRICE_SHARE_OF_REDO_COST` → 0.10 (so the derived cap →

`packages/carpool-core/src/pricing.test.ts`. No figure in `docs/AB-MEASUREMENT.md`
